# Gap register — what a hardware signatory would still ask (2026-10-18)

Goal: a Ukraine Defense proof-of-concept strong enough that an evaluator
releases flight hardware. The standard for this file: the evaluator finds
nothing we did not already find, measure, and either close or state as
physics. Every row is one gap, how it was found, its status and the
closing action. Numbers come from the named tests and runs; nothing here
is estimated.

Status key: **closed** (measured, in the default ctest or the Gazebo
evidence), **open/in progress** (work queued or running), **physics**
(not closable by any controller; stated as an airframe requirement),
**hardware** (needs a physical board or airframe).

| G8 | Tick budget on slower silicon at the shipped geometries | Track D; restated 2026-10-18 | **closed for what flies**, open for learning on | The earlier figures (197,440 → 40,640 instr) were the 64 × 64 synthetic twin, not a shipped prior. At real geometry (96 hidden, QEMU icount, `docs/performance.md`): the **flight default** (832 bits, inference only, plasticity off) worst tick **125,200** instr on M7, 125,280 on M3 → 1 kHz needs ≥ 125 MHz at 1 cycle/instr, ≥ 250 MHz at 2 (600 MHz M7: 4.8× / 2.4× margin; a ≤ 120 MHz M3 does not fit). Worst tick on M7, plain / dense: 331,480 / 408,760; amortised 114,400 / **138,640** (M3 139,120). The zero-trace skip was measured and not kept: it cuts the plain twin to 196,440 but raises the dense worst tick. Still open: the dense twin is a measured stress, not an analytic bound.
